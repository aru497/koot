485 Visa Fee Increase 2026: It Just Doubled to A$4,600

By Koott7 min readUpdated July 2026
Quick answer

From 1 March 2026, the base application charge for a primary subclass 485 (Temporary Graduate) visa is A$4,600 — double the previous A$2,300. Secondary applicants pay A$2,300 (18+) or A$1,160 (under 18). The regional "second" 485 extension is far cheaper at about A$1,810. Always confirm the live fee on the Department of Home Affairs website before you apply.

If you are an Indian or Kerala student planning to work in Australia after your degree, the number just changed on you. On 1 March 2026, the government doubled the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) application fee overnight — from A$2,300 to A$4,600. Here is exactly what went up, who is affected, the one option that is still cheap, and how to plan around it.

How much is the 485 visa fee now?

As of 1 March 2026, the base application charge for a primary applicant for the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) is A$4,600. That is a 100% increase — the fee literally doubled in a single day, from the A$2,300 that applied since July 2025. The change was announced with little warning, which is why so many soon-to-graduate students were caught off guard.

This is the base application charge only. Your total cost can be higher once you add health checks, biometrics, police certificates, English test fees (if required) and Overseas Student Health Cover. Budget for the full picture, not just the headline number.

The fee timeline: how we got to A$4,600

The 485 fee did not jump in one move — it climbed in steps, then doubled:

Effective datePrimary applicant feeChange
Before Feb 2025A$1,945—
1 February 2025A$2,235+A$290
1 July 2025A$2,300+A$65
1 March 2026A$4,600+A$2,300 (doubled)

In just over a year, the primary 485 fee went from A$1,945 to A$4,600 — roughly 2.4× more expensive. Fees can also be indexed each 1 July, so treat these figures as a snapshot and confirm the current charge before you lodge.

485 fees by applicant type

If you are bringing family, the base charges from 1 March 2026 are:

ApplicantBase application charge
Primary applicantA$4,600
Secondary applicant (18 and over)A$2,300
Secondary applicant (under 18)A$1,160
Regional "second" 485 (extra stream)A$1,810

So a graduate applying with a partner now faces roughly A$6,900 in base charges alone (A$4,600 + A$2,300) — before add-ons. That is a serious line item in any student budget.

The regional exception — now a bigger advantage

Here is the part most panic coverage skips. The Second Post-Higher Education Work stream — the additional 485 visa available to graduates who studied at a regional campus — carries a much lower charge of about A$1,810. That is well under half the standard fee.

In other words, the fee hike makes the regional pathway relatively even more attractive: you get one or two extra years of post-study work rights, and the extension itself is a fraction of the standard cost. If you are still choosing where to study, this is one more reason to look beyond Sydney and Melbourne.

Worth knowing

Cities like Adelaide, Newcastle, Wollongong, Hobart and the Gold Coast count as regional for migration purposes — and their universities often cost less in tuition too. We break this down in our 485 regional bonus guide and why regional Australia guide.

Who is exempt from the increase?

The fee increase does not apply to passport holders from 13 Pacific and Timor-Leste nations: Federated States of Micronesia, Fiji, Kiribati, Nauru, Palau, Papua New Guinea, Marshall Islands, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Timor-Leste, Tonga, Tuvalu and Vanuatu. For Indian passport holders, unfortunately, the full A$4,600 applies.

Why did the fee double?

According to the Department of Home Affairs, the increase is meant to "restore integrity" to the post-study work route. The government's view is that the 485 had become a default stop-gap for graduates with no clear long-term plan, so a higher fee is intended to deter non-genuine applicants. The extra revenue is earmarked to fund visa-integrity measures — the Genuine Student assessment, an expanded compliance division, and real-time data-matching with the Australian Taxation Office.

Whether or not you agree with the rationale, the practical takeaway is the same: the 485 is now a considered, higher-stakes decision — not an automatic one. Applying with a real plan for work and skills that Australia actually needs matters more than ever.

How does it compare globally?

At A$4,600, Australia's post-study work visa is now one of the most expensive in the English-speaking world — reported to be more than 10× the cost of a comparable visa in Canada, around 3× New Zealand, and roughly 2× the United Kingdom. That gap is prompting some students to weigh other destinations, and it triggered a grass-roots campaign against the sudden change.

That said, the visa fee is a one-off cost against potentially two to four years of Australian salary and work experience. It is a bigger upfront number, not necessarily a worse long-term deal — especially if your course leads to an in-demand, higher-paying field.

What this means for Indian & Kerala students

  • Budget the real number. Add A$4,600 (plus health cover and add-ons) to your post-graduation costs from day one. Our funding guide and visa cost tools can help you plan.
  • Weigh the regional route. A regional campus can mean lower tuition, an extra 485 year, and a cheaper extension (~A$1,810). See regional cost of living.
  • Pick a course Australia is hiring for. A higher visa fee stings less when your field pays well and has real shortages. That is exactly what our free Course Advisor matches you to — using real Skills Priority List data.
  • Apply deliberately. With integrity checks tightening, a genuine study-to-work plan is now the safest application.
